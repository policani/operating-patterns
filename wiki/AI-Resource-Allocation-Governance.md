# AI Resource Allocation Governance

## Operating problem

AI initiatives can create pressure to experiment everywhere at once. Without governance, organizations may spend attention, data access, tool capacity, and change effort on work that is interesting but not clearly tied to business value, risk reduction, cycle time, or decision quality.

## Pattern

AI resource allocation should be governed like an operating portfolio. The useful question is not only whether AI can be applied, but where it should be applied, what human review is required, what workflow changes are needed, and how value will be measured.

## Typical interventions

- Separate exploration from governed adoption
- Map AI use cases to business value, cycle time, risk control, or decision quality
- Identify human review points
- Clarify data sensitivity and access constraints
- Define operating owners for adoption
- Compare effort, value, risk, and repeatability across use cases

## What good looks like

- Fewer disconnected AI experiments
- Better use of scarce AI, data, and change capacity
- Clearer human-in-the-loop expectations
- Stronger adoption paths
- More defensible value realization

## Evidence pattern

This pattern reflects generalized work with AI-enabled operating practices, governed project spaces, workflow orchestration, and business-value allocation. Specific private workspaces, prompts, systems, employers, and internal details are intentionally omitted.

## Demonstrated capabilities

AI operating governance, resource allocation, workflow orchestration, value realization, risk-aware adoption.
