# Intake Discipline

## Operating problem

Distributed organizations often collect work through too many channels. Requests arrive as escalations, executive asks, roadmap assumptions, team commitments, customer pressure, compliance needs, or informal priority claims.

When everything enters the system as urgent, the organization loses the ability to distinguish value, risk, dependency, and timing.

## Pattern

The useful work is to separate intake from prioritization. Intake should make work visible. Prioritization should decide whether the work deserves capacity, when it should move, and what tradeoffs it creates.

## Typical interventions

- Create a common intake language
- Separate request capture from approval
- Identify decision rights
- Require enough context to evaluate value and risk
- Tag dependency, urgency, funding, readiness, and executive visibility
- Keep a visible record of accepted, deferred, blocked, and rejected work

## What good looks like

- Fewer hidden commitments
- Cleaner escalation paths
- Less duplicate work
- Better sequencing conversations
- Stronger executive confidence in the portfolio signal

## Evidence pattern

This pattern reflects repeated work in enterprise portfolio and delivery governance settings. Specific employers, dates, internal systems, and operating details are intentionally generalized or omitted.

## Demonstrated capabilities

Portfolio governance, intake discipline, decision rights, signal integrity, executive decision support.
