# Proof Patterns

Proof patterns describe the shape of evidence without exposing employer-specific details. They are useful because many enterprise roles need the same underlying capabilities even when the industry, title, or system is different.

They should be read as anonymized lessons learned, not as invented examples. The public version removes identifying details while keeping the transferable operating logic visible.

## How these differ from case studies

A case study usually tells a named story. A proof pattern shows a transferable operating capability:

- What kind of ambiguity was present
- What signal needed to improve
- What governance structure helped
- What type of outcome became easier to manage

This keeps the public material useful without exposing confidential facts.
