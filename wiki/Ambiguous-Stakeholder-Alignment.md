# Ambiguous Stakeholder Alignment

## Situation pattern

Multiple teams care about the outcome, but they do not share the same vocabulary, incentives, urgency, or risk tolerance. Each group may be making reasonable decisions locally while the broader operating system becomes harder to steer.

## Useful move

Create a shared operating frame before trying to force agreement. Alignment usually improves when stakeholders can see the decision, dependency, risk, and tradeoff structure in the same place.

## Signals to look for

- Repeated escalation without decision closure
- Different definitions of success
- Hidden veto points
- Unclear ownership
- Competing urgency claims
- Status meetings that do not resolve tradeoffs

## Practices

- Name the decision that needs alignment
- Identify which stakeholders own, advise, approve, or are affected
- Separate facts from interpretations
- Capture unresolved assumptions
- Make tradeoffs explicit enough to discuss

## Demonstrated capabilities

Stakeholder alignment, decision rights, executive facilitation, operating model design, governance discipline.
