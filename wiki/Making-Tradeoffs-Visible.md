# Making Tradeoffs Visible

## Situation pattern

Organizations often describe competing work as a prioritization problem when it is really a tradeoff problem. The issue is not only which initiative is more important. It is what the organization gives up, delays, risks, or protects by choosing one path over another.

## Useful move

Make tradeoffs visible enough that leaders can own them. A good tradeoff frame shows options, constraints, consequences, and reversibility.

## Signals to look for

- Every request is described as critical
- Capacity conflicts are hidden inside team-level planning
- Strategic priority and execution readiness are mixed together
- Leaders approve work without seeing the opportunity cost
- Teams absorb tradeoffs without explicit direction

## Practices

- Identify constrained resources and decision capacity
- Show what each option enables, delays, or risks
- Separate reversible and hard-to-reverse choices
- Connect tradeoffs to business outcomes
- Record the decision and revisit it when conditions change

## Demonstrated capabilities

Executive decision support, resource sequencing, portfolio governance, value realization, business impact framing.
