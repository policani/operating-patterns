# Release Readiness and UAT

## Operating problem

Release readiness can become a checklist exercise when teams focus only on whether tasks are complete. In complex environments, the harder question is whether the business, users, systems, data, controls, support model, and decision owners are actually ready for change.

## Pattern

Readiness should be treated as an operating signal. UAT, deployment governance, and release decisions should expose unresolved risk before it becomes production disruption or adoption failure.

## Typical interventions

- Define readiness criteria before the final release window
- Separate testing completion from business acceptance
- Track unresolved decisions, defects, training gaps, and support impacts
- Make go, no-go, and conditional-go choices explicit
- Connect release risk to business consequence
- Preserve an escalation path for blockers

## What good looks like

- Cleaner release decisions
- Stronger business acceptance
- Earlier detection of production risk
- Better support readiness
- Less last-minute escalation

## Evidence pattern

This pattern reflects generalized work in UAT, deployment readiness, finance systems, platform operations, and release governance. Specific systems, releases, dates, and internal criteria are intentionally omitted.

## Demonstrated capabilities

Release readiness, UAT governance, deployment coordination, business acceptance, risk translation.
